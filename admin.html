1. Social Influence

Types of Conformity

Internalisation: Deepest level; person genuinely accepts group norms. Result in private and public change.

Compliance: Superficial; "going along with others" in public but privately disagreeing.

Identification: Conforming to a group we value to be part of it.

Variables (Asch)

  • Group Size: Conformity rose to 31.8% with 3 confederates.
  • Unanimity: A dissenter reduced conformity by 25%.
  • Task Difficulty: Difficulty increase leads to ISI.

Obedience (Milgram)

  • Proximity: Higher when closer to authority.
  • Location: Prestige (Yale) increases obedience.
  • Uniform: Laboratory coat = legitimacy.

Resistance & Minority Influence

Social Support: Presence of others who resist helps break unanimity.

Locus of Control (LOC): Internals believe they control their own destiny; more likely to resist.

Minority Influence: Requires Consistency (not changing view), Commitment (Augmentation principle), and Flexibility (negotiation).

2. Research Methods

Experimental Methods

  • Lab: High control, low ecological validity.
  • Field: Natural setting, high validity, low control.
  • Natural: IV varies naturally (e.g., impact of TV).
  • Quasi: Based on existing differences (e.g., gender).

Observations

  • Covert vs Overt: Hidden vs Open.
  • Participant: Researcher joins group.
  • Sampling: Event (count behaviors) or Time (observe at intervals).

Self-Report & Others

Interviews: Structured (fixed questions) vs Unstructured (conversation-like).

Content Analysis: Indirect study of communication (coding/thematic analysis).

Case Studies: In-depth investigation of an individual or small group.

3. Scientific Processes

Foundations

Hypotheses: Directional (states nature of difference) vs Non-directional.

Sampling: Random, Systematic, Stratified, Opportunity, Volunteer.

Pilot Studies: Small scale trial to check procedure/feasibility.

Control & Ethics

Reliability: Consistency. Measured by Test-retest or Inter-observer.

Validity: Truthfulness. Face, Concurrent, Ecological, Temporal.

Ethics: Informed consent, Deception, Protection from harm, Privacy.

Features of Science

Objectivity: Bias-free.

Replicability: Can be repeated.

Falsifiability: Can be proven wrong.

Paradigms: Shared set of assumptions (shifts occur over time).
